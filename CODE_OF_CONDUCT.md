# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to each other. Discuss the code, not the person writing it.

## What is expected

- Keep criticism about the change: what it does, what it breaks, what it costs.
- Assume the other person is trying to help, including when they are wrong.
- Accept that a maintainer may turn a change down and still be glad you sent
  it. A narrow tool stays useful by saying no more often than yes.
- Remember that most people here are writing in a second language and working
  on this in their own time.

## What is not

Harassment, insults, or personal attacks. Sexualised language or imagery.
Publishing someone's private information. Deliberately derailing a thread.
Sustained disruption after being asked to stop.

## Scope

This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, and anywhere else the
project is discussed.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may delete comments, close threads, or block accounts, without
discussion where the case is obvious.

To report behaviour you should not have to put up with, open a private report
from the **Security** tab of this repository, which is a thread only the
maintainers can read. Serious cases belong with GitHub as well: see
[Reporting abuse or spam](https://docs.github.com/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam).

Reports are handled in confidence. A maintainer who is the subject of a report
has no part in handling it.
